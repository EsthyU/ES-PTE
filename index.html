    RW Priority 1

    Fill in the Blanks

    Feeds Reading and Writing simultaneously. Your highest-leverage task. A correct word spelled wrong scores zero.

    Method

    1. Identify the grammatical slot before the meaning. Noun, verb form, adjective, adverb.
    2. Check for a collocation trigger. Most blanks want a fixed partner, not a synonym.
    3. Read the full sentence back with your word in place.
    4. Spell it, then check the spelling. Never leave a blank.

    R Reading

    Re-order Paragraphs

    Partial credit for correct adjacent pairs, so locking two confident pairs beats agonising over the whole sequence. Hard cap: two minutes per item.

    Cannot be first

    Any paragraph opening with a back-reference or connector:

    thisthesesuchittheythe formerthe latterhoweverthereforemoreoverin additionanotheras a resultfor exampleconsequentlyneverthelessby contrastsubsequentlyfinallydespite this

    Chaining signals

    You seeIt means
    Pronoun with no antecedent in that paragraphAntecedent is in the paragraph immediately before
    Definite article on first mention — “the scheme”The scheme was introduced earlier
    Indefinite article — “a new scheme”This is the introduction, so it comes early
    Full name, then surname onlyFull name comes first
    A general claimA specific example usually follows
    A questionThe answer is the next paragraph

    Click paragraphs in the order you think they belong.

    The skeleton

    Dear [Name / Sir or Madam],

    I am writing to [purpose, one clause, tied directly to the prompt].

    [Prompt point 1.] [Prompt point 2, using a subordinate clause.] [Prompt point 3 — usually a request, using a modal.]

    I would be grateful if you could [specific action]. Please let me know if you require any further information.

    Kind regards,
    Esther

    Type it until it takes under 60 seconds. That leaves seven minutes for content and one for proofing.

    Summary tasks

    Summarize Written Text

    Ten minutes, 25–50 words. Outside that range scores zero. Target 38–44.

    Template A — one complex sentence
    “Although [concession], the passage argues that [main claim], demonstrating that [key evidence or implication].”

    Template B — two sentences
    “[Main claim: subject–verb–object.] This is supported by [evidence one] and [evidence two], suggesting that [implication].”

    Summarize Spoken Text

    Eight minutes, 20–30 words. Under 5 or over 40 scores zero. Target 24–28. One main idea plus one qualifier, nothing else.

    Template
    “The speaker explains that [main idea], noting that [one supporting point or consequence].” That is 8 words of scaffold, leaving 12–22 for content.

    Speaking — maintenance only

    You scored 90. That is the ceiling. Fifteen minutes once a week: 5 Read Aloud, 5 Repeat Sentence, 2 Describe Image, 2 Respond to a Situation.

    Describe Image frame — 25s prep, 40s response, fill all 40:
    “This [chart type] presents information about [topic]. The most striking feature is that [highest/lowest/biggest change]. In contrast, [second data point]. There is also a noticeable [trend] in [third element]. Overall, the image suggests that [restate headline pattern].”

    Respond to a Situation frame — 40s response:
    “[Acknowledge appropriately]. [State your position or apology directly.] [One brief reason.] [Concrete alternative or next step.] [Polite close.]”

    Three rules that protect a 90: never self-correct in Read Aloud; never pause three seconds, because the recording can close; fill the whole response window.
